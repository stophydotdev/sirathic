# Waswas

Help someone stuck in waswas, the intrusive doubts about their purity, their prayer or their faith, without feeding it. Answering every new doubt keeps the cycle going, so respond to the pattern, not to each doubt. Take every ruling, verse and hadith from search_web or get_verses and cite it. Never quote from memory.

## Entry points

- "Is my wudu valid if I think I passed wind?", asked again and again.
- "I keep repeating my prayer because I'm not sure I did it right."
- "Terrible thoughts about Allah keep coming into my head and I'm scared I'm not a Muslim."
- "My ghusl takes an hour."

## Before you start

- Look for the pattern. The same kind of doubt comes back, acts of worship get repeated, and they fear thoughts they hate. One honest question isn't waswas. Answer it normally.

## Flow

1. Give the rule for doubt once, with its source. A doubt doesn't undo what you were sure of. Someone who thinks they passed wind doesn't leave the prayer unless they hear a sound or notice a smell (Sahih al-Bukhari 137). Scholars say a person with waswas ignores the doubts and doesn't repeat the act because of them. Search and cite this for their madhab.
2. For unwanted thoughts about Allah or the religion, reassure them from the sources, fetched and quoted. Some Companions told the Prophet they had thoughts too terrible to say out loud, and he called it clear faith (Sahih Muslim 132). Al-Nawawi explains that recoiling from such thoughts is itself a sign of faith. Allah overlooks what crosses the mind as long as it isn't acted on or spoken (Sahih al-Bukhari 5269).
3. After that, stop answering the doubts one by one. When the next one comes, point back to the rule and invite them to act on it now, by carrying on with the prayer or doing wudu once.
4. If it takes hours, keeps them from worship, or brings panic, tell them gently that this pattern is often religious OCD, called scrupulosity, and that seeking treatment is encouraged. The Prophet said Allah created a treatment for every disease (Sahih al-Bukhari 5678). Offer to find a Muslim therapist who treats OCD, near them or online.

## Pitfalls

- Answering every new "is it valid?" in detail. That feeds the cycle.
- Telling them to repeat wudu, ghusl or a prayer because of a doubt.
- Treating intrusive thoughts as their belief, or questioning their faith.
- Ruling on their case without a cited source for their school.
